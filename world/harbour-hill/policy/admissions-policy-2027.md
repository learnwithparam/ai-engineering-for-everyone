# Harbour Hill School: Admissions Policy for Year 7, entry in September 2027

Fictional. Harbour Hill School, Kestrel Bay, and every person in this folder are made up for a course.

Status: current. Agreed by the governing board. This policy replaces the 2026 policy.

## 1. About this policy

1.1 Harbour Hill School is a state secondary school for students aged 11 to 18. The governing board is the
admission authority. This policy says how we decide who gets a place in Year 7.

1.2 A person on the admissions team makes every admission decision. No computer system, scoring tool or
AI system decides who is offered a place. Tools may help staff read forms and draft letters. A named member
of staff checks and signs every offer, refusal and letter.

1.3 If this policy and any letter, email or web page disagree, this policy is right.

## 2. How many places there are

2.1 The school has 180 places in Year 7. This number is called the published admission number.

2.2 If 180 or fewer children apply, every child who applies gets a place.

2.3 If more than 180 children apply, we use the order in section 4 to decide who gets a place.

## 3. How to apply

3.1 Parents apply through the Kestrel Bay Council common application form. Parents may name up to three
schools in order of preference.

3.2 The preference rank does not change a child's place in our order. We only use it to decide which offer a
family gets when the child qualifies for more than one school.

3.3 We use the information on the form. If something is missing, we ask the family. We do not guess.

3.4 A child with an Education, Health and Care Plan (EHCP) that names Harbour Hill School is given a place
before section 4 is used. This lowers the number of places left for everyone else.

## 4. The order we use when there are more applicants than places

After children with an EHCP naming the school (3.4), places are given in this order.

4.1 **Looked-after children.** Children in the care of a local authority, and children who were in care and
were then adopted or placed under a special guardianship or child arrangements order.

4.2 **Children of staff.** Children of a member of staff who has worked at the school for two years or more
when the application is made, or who was hired for a post where there is a skills shortage.

4.3 **Siblings.** Children who have a sibling on roll at Harbour Hill School in Years 7 to 11 when the child
would start. A sibling means a brother or sister, a half brother or sister, an adopted brother or sister, a
foster brother or sister, or a stepbrother or stepsister, who lives at the same address as the child. A sibling
at Harbour Hill Primary School, or in our sixth form, does not count.

4.4 **Distance.** All other children, in order of distance from home to school. The nearest child comes first.

## 5. How we measure distance

5.1 Distance is a straight line from the child's home address to the main gate of the school, measured by the
council's map system. We show it in kilometres to two decimal places.

5.2 **Home address.** The home address is where the child lives. If a child lives at two addresses, we use the
address where the child sleeps on most school nights, from Sunday night to Thursday night. If the time is equal,
a person on the admissions team asks the family for proof and decides. We will not use a business address, a
grandparent's address or a relative's address unless the child lives there.

5.3 If we cannot tell the home address from the form, we mark the application as needing a person and we
contact the family.

## 6. Tie-break

6.1 If two children are the same distance from school and only one place is left, we draw lots. A person who is
not part of the school supervises the draw.

6.2 Twins, triplets and other children from a multiple birth are all offered a place, even if this takes the
school over 180.

## 7. Waiting list

7.1 Every child who applied and was not offered a place goes on the waiting list.

7.2 The waiting list is kept in the order in section 4. It is not kept in the order families joined it.

7.3 When a place becomes free, we offer it to the child at the top of the waiting list. A person on the
admissions team checks the list and signs the offer.

7.4 A child's position on the list can go up or down when other children join or leave it.

7.5 The waiting list stays open until the end of the autumn term of Year 7.

## 8. Late applications

8.1 An application that arrives after the closing date is late. We look at late applications only after all
applications that arrived on time, unless there is a good reason, such as a family moving into Kestrel Bay.

## 9. Withdrawing a place

9.1 A family can turn down or give up a place. They must tell us in writing from the email address or postal
address on the application. If a message comes from an email address we do not have on file, we do not act on
it. A person on the admissions team contacts the family on the details we have.

9.2 We may withdraw an offer if it was made because of false or misleading information, such as a false home
address.

## 10. Appeals

10.1 Every family refused a place can appeal to an independent appeal panel. The decision letter must say how to
appeal. See the appeals guide.

## 11. Fair treatment

11.1 We do not use a child's name, gender, race, religion, disability or health, other than an EHCP as set out in
3.4, or a parent's job, other than in 4.2, to decide a place.

11.2 We treat every application with care and keep children's information private.
